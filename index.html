The Koda Noble Style Guide

Dress Well, Effortlessly

Everything a well-dressed man should know — colours, fit, fabrics, occasions, and the small details that separate good from unforgettable.

A Word From Our Head Tailor

"Style is not what you wear — it's how you wear it."

A man does not need fifty pieces. He needs ten good ones, worn with intention. Below is our complete guide to building a wardrobe that works — for the boardroom, the wedding, and the weekend.

— Head Tailor, Koda Noble

Chapter One

Colour Combinations

Start With A Foundation Of Three

The men who always look sharp have figured out one thing: navy, charcoal and white form the backbone of every good wardrobe. Everything else — camel, burgundy, olive, brass — is an accent that plays beautifully against those three.

Start every outfit by picking one of these three as your anchor. If your suit is navy, your shirt should be white or very pale blue. If your suit is charcoal, almost anything goes — but keep the shirt neutral.

Six Combinations That Never Fail

The Boardroom

Navy suit · White shirt · Black shoes. The safest, strongest combination in menswear. Worn with a simple silk tie, this is authority without effort.

The Evening

Charcoal suit · Pale blue shirt · Burgundy tie. Warmer and richer. Perfect for gallery openings, dinner dates, and evening events.

The Summer Wedding

Beige linen suit · White shirt · Cognac derby. Warm, breathable, effortlessly elegant. Skip the tie — open collar does the work.

The Weekend

Navy blazer · Stone chino · Brown suede loafer. The most versatile look in menswear. Lunch, shopping, long drive — all with one formula.

The Celebration

Charcoal suit · White shirt · Brass tie. Golden accent adds a touch of ceremony. Perfect for engagements, award dinners, milestones.

The Countryside

Olive jacket · White shirt · Brown leather boot. Earth tones, natural textures, easy weekend elegance.

Chapter Two

Fit Is Everything

A $200 Suit That Fits Beats A $2,000 Suit That Doesn't

Before you spend money on fabric or brand names — get the fit right. A correctly fitted suit makes you look taller, leaner, and more confident. Here is how to check yours in 30 seconds.

  • ◆Shoulders: The seam should sit exactly where your shoulder ends. Not beyond, not inside.
  • ◆Chest: You should be able to fit one flat hand between chest and jacket. Any more, it's too loose.
  • ◆Sleeve: Show ¼ to ½ inch of shirt cuff. Sleeve should end at the wrist bone.
  • ◆Jacket length: Should cover your seat — reaching the middle of your hands.
  • ◆Trouser break: One clean fold where trouser meets shoe. That's it.

Three Fits — Choose Your Silhouette

Slim Fit

The Sharp Silhouette

Close to the body, minimal drape. Best for lean builds. Sharpest look for young men under 35 and for evening wear.

Tailored Fit

The Balanced Classic

Defined but comfortable. Structured shoulders, gentle waist suppression. The safest fit for men of any age or build.

Classic Fit

The Generous Drape

Roomier through the chest and shoulders. Comfortable for broader builds and for men who prefer an easy, unhurried look.

Chapter Three

The Details That Matter

01

Shoes

Match your shoes to your belt — always. Black shoes for evening and formal. Brown/cognac for day and country. Suede loafers for warm weekends.

02

The Belt

Slim leather, no big buckle. Width should match your trouser loop. If you're wearing a suit, consider braces instead — no belt at all is the sharpest choice.

03

The Watch

Simple dial. Leather strap for formal, steel for everyday. Avoid oversized faces — 38 to 40mm is the sweet spot for most wrists.

04

The Pocket Square

Never match your tie — complement it. White linen is the universal choice. One bold colour at a time. Fold: presidential (flat) for formal, puff for casual.

Chapter Four

Build A Wardrobe

Ten essentials. Everything else is a nice-to-have. Start with these and you'll never look out of place.

01

The Navy Suit

Your most versatile investment. Works for business, weddings, and dinners. Choose Super 120s or 150s wool — smooth, resilient, timeless.

02

The Charcoal Suit

Slightly more formal than navy. Wear it to evening events, board meetings, and serious occasions. The suit that says you mean business.

03

Three White Shirts & Two Blue

White oxford for day, white poplin for formal, white linen for summer. Pale blue is the second-colour that goes with everything.

04

The Navy Blazer

The most versatile jacket. Worn with chinos it's smart-casual; with wool trousers it's a proper suit replacement. Own one. Wear it weekly.

05

Two Pairs of Good Trousers

Charcoal wool for formal settings, stone cotton for weekend. Get them hemmed with a single, clean break.

06

The Overcoat

Double-faced cashmere in charcoal. Winter elegance in one garment. Knee-length is correct — not too long, not too short.

07

Black Oxford Shoes

The most formal shoe a man can own. Wear them with suits, dinners, weddings, everything serious. Buy the best you can afford.

08

Brown Derby or Loafer

Your daytime shoe. Cognac, walnut or stone suede. Wears beautifully with chinos, navy blazers, and beige linen.

09

Three Silk Ties

Navy (safest), burgundy (warmest), brass (for celebration). Hand-rolled silk, 7–8 cm wide. No novelty prints, ever.

10

A Navy Overcoat-Trouser Combination

One look that works in every cold-weather situation: navy overcoat, charcoal trousers, white shirt, black oxfords. Learn it by heart.

The Final Word

When In Doubt, Keep It Simple

The best-dressed men are not the ones with the most clothes. They are the ones who know what suits them, bought well, and wore it with quiet confidence. Style is a habit — build it slowly, own it fully.

Chapter OneThe Origins
Our Story

A Quiet Obsession With Detail

Koda Noble was born in 1998 in a small atelier above a tailor's shop in Addis Ababa — the same workshop where our founder learned to wield a needle at the age of fourteen.

What began as a single commission for a merchant's wedding suit grew, over two decades, into a house with ateliers across three cities. Today our garments are worn in boardrooms in Milan and by grooms in Addis. The tools have changed. The obsession hasn't.

We still cut every pattern by hand. We still sew the first buttonhole on every jacket ourselves. We still believe a man's wardrobe is not a collection — it is a diary.

From Fibre to Finish

The Process

Step 01

Fabric Selection

We travel to Biella, Huddersfield and Kyoto each season. Out of thousands of bolts, fewer than forty make it into our collection.

Step 02

Pattern Cutting

Each pattern is drafted by hand on paper, refined over decades. A single jacket block may go through 90 iterations before it earns the Koda mark.

Step 03

Hand Assembly

A single suit passes through the hands of seven artisans. Floating canvas, hand-padded lapels, horn buttons — the way it was done a century ago.

Step 04

Final Fitting

Every garment is pressed, inspected and signed by the master tailor who finished it. It is then wrapped in tissue and sent to you.

Commitment

Made With Conscience

Every fabric we use is traceable to the farm, mill or spinner that produced it. Every atelier we work with is audited twice a year for fair wages and safe conditions.

Our zero-waste pattern cutting recycles 92% of off-cuts back into linings, pocket squares and buttons. What remains is composted or donated to local design schools.
